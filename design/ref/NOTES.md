## What the native app does that the web doesn't (observed 2026-08-10,
## Pacific St plate — hold the renderer to this bar)
1. **Individual street trees** — soft, slightly irregular organic canopy
   blobs with a hint of volume (darker core), not geometric circles. Trees
   sit on sidewalks AND courtyards; density reads as truth, not decoration.
2. **Curb geometry is honored** — the bump-out on Pacific St renders as a
   smooth confident curve. Curb extensions, corner radii, and jogs are
   drawn, not simplified away. (User called this out specifically.)
3. **Crosswalks** — zebra stripes at intersections, aligned to the crossing.
4. **One-way arrows** — small chevrons on the carriageway, spaced along the
   block, correct direction.
5. **Roadbed text markings** — "SCHOOL" set along the lane like real NYC
   thermoplast. Street names set ON the road ribbon, white/letterspaced.
6. **Building massing** — rounded corners, courtyards punched out, subtle
   per-building value variation within one hue family.
7. **Play/park surfaces** — colored honestly (green court, terracotta
   surround) but muted; never poster-bright.
8. **Chrome discipline** — scale bar quiet at top; controls cluster at
   edges; the map owns every pixel that matters.

## Palette
Ours is the light family (design/tokens.json), not the app's dark navy.
Steal the app's *structure* (trees, crosswalks, arrows, markings, curves)
and the web plates' *light palette temperature*.
